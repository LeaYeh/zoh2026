This is a supercompute hackathon — the expectation is a real, fine-tuned model, not a prompt-engineering demo. Three tracks, three sponsors. Below is a breakdown of what each partner does, what the challenge will likely look like, and how to win it.

TRACK 01

AI in Industry

PARTNER: INFINEON TECHNOLOGIES · Manufacturing · Operations · Industrial systems
Who they are One of Europe's largest semiconductor manufacturers — chips for automotive, IoT, and industrial systems. Their internal AI work focuses on sensor-based condition monitoring, wafer and PCB defect detection, and process parameter optimization on factory lines.
Likely challenge Expect either industrial images (surface defects on chips or PCBs) or multivariate sensor time series (motor vibration, temperature, pressure). The real difficulty: labels will be scarce — defects are rare in the wild, so you can't just throw a supervised model at it.
Winning approach Fine-tune a ViT-B/16 or EfficientNet-B4 on defect images using ImageNet pretrained weights + LoRA for efficient adaptation. For the label-scarce case, use PatchCore (anomalib) — it only needs normal samples and flags anything that deviates. Add a GradCAM heatmap overlay: judges can see exactly where the model detects the defect. That visual sells the pitch.
Tech stack
PyTorch + timm ViT / EfficientNet PatchCore (anomalib) LoRA / PEFT GradCAM Albumentations

WHY PICK THIS

  • Vision fine-tuning is well-documented — strong Kaggle reference material (Severstal, MVTec AD)
  • PatchCore handles zero defect labels — lower data risk
  • GradCAM makes an inherently visual, memorable demo
  • A100 is well-used: ViT-B/16 full fine-tune benefits from it

WATCH OUT FOR

  • If the data is sensor time series instead of images, you need a different model branch ready
  • Industrial datasets can be messy and unlabeled — EDA eats into build time
TRACK 02

AI in Insurance

PARTNER: UNIQA · Underwriting · Claims · Risk modeling
Who they are Austria's largest insurer, operating across health, property, and auto insurance in 18 countries. Their AI priorities: risk scoring for underwriting (who gets charged what premium), claims severity prediction, and fraud detection. Regulatory explainability is non-negotiable in their industry.
Likely challenge A structured tabular dataset — customer attributes plus claims history — with a regression or classification target (predict risk level, premium amount, or claim probability). Expect imbalanced classes, many categorical features, and possibly some free-text fields (claim descriptions).
Winning approach A LightGBM + deep tabular model ensemble with heavy feature engineering. The decisive edge isn't the model — it's SHAP explainability: insurance judges are legally required to justify individual decisions, so a SHAP waterfall plot per prediction is directly relevant to their real workflow. Bonus: encode any text fields (claim descriptions, medical codes) with a sentence-transformer and feed into the tabular model.
Tech stack
LightGBM / XGBoost TabPFN / FT-Transformer SHAP Optuna sentence-transformers category_encoders

WHY PICK THIS

  • Fastest path to a working, submittable baseline (LightGBM trains in minutes)
  • Classic Kaggle comps (Porto Seguro, Allstate Claims) map directly — tons of reference notebooks
  • SHAP visuals are easy to produce and make a strong pitch
  • Most beginner-friendly track technically

WATCH OUT FOR

  • A100 is overkill for pure tabular ML — hard to leverage the compute advantage
  • Many teams will converge on the same LightGBM baseline — differentiation is harder
  • Without creative feature engineering, models plateau quickly
TRACK 03

AI in Forecasting

PARTNER: SYBILION · Probabilistic forecasting · Decision agent layer
Who they are Vienna-based startup, just closed a $4.2M seed round. Sybilion builds a decision layer for industrial companies — filtering 1 trillion external signals (commodity prices, freight rates, energy futures, weather anomalies, trade flows) into concrete procurement and margin-protection recommendations. The core insight: industrial teams don't lack data, they lack clarity about which signals matter and when to commit.
Likely challenge Given historical time series plus external signals, produce a probabilistic forecast — not a single number but a range (e.g. 10th / 50th / 90th percentile). Then build an agent layer that interprets those uncertainty bounds and outputs a structured business decision: "buy by D+3, confidence 80%, downside risk €X if delayed."
Winning approach Two-layer architecture: (1) fine-tuned PatchTST or Chronos (Amazon's pretrained time series foundation model) for probabilistic forecasting with calibrated uncertainty, using Conformal Prediction for coverage guarantees; (2) an LLM agent that reads the forecast output and generates structured recommendations with explicit trade-offs. This is the only track where a fine-tuned forecasting model and an LLM genuinely need to work together — which means A100 time is fully justified and the architecture is hard to replicate quickly.
Tech stack
PatchTST / Chronos Quantile regression Conformal prediction HuggingFace Time Series LLM agent + tool calling LightGBM (baseline)

WHY PICK THIS

  • Highest ceiling — two-model system is hard for other teams to replicate in 36 hours
  • Best use of A100: fine-tune a TS foundation model + run an LLM agent
  • Sybilion is an early-stage startup actively looking for novel approaches
  • M5 Uncertainty + Optiver Kaggle notebooks map directly to the core task
  • The agent layer makes the demo interactive and immediately intuitive

WATCH OUT FOR

  • Two moving parts — if one breaks, the whole demo breaks
  • Probabilistic forecasting + agent design is harder to debug under pressure
  • Need at least one person comfortable with both time series and LLM APIs

TLDR FOR TEAM DISCUSSION

Track 01 (Infineon) is the safest technical bet — vision fine-tuning is well-practiced, the demo is visual and intuitive, and Kaggle prep material maps directly to the likely challenge.

Track 02 (UNIQA) gets you to a working model fastest but is the hardest to differentiate — LightGBM baselines are easy, and A100s are underutilized on pure tabular work.

Track 03 (Sybilion) has the highest upside. It's the only track where a forecasting model and an LLM agent working in tandem is genuinely the expected architecture — which means the full compute stack is justified, and competitors can't easily replicate a two-layer system in 36 hours. Higher risk, higher reward.
